# AP-INC-002 Operational Incident Response and Review

## 1. Purpose and Scope  
This policy establishes the procedures for the timely reporting, coordination, and review of operational incidents at AeroPolicy Airport. It ensures that all incidents are managed in a manner that prioritizes safety, compliance, and continuous improvement. The scope of this policy includes all incidents that occur on airside, landside, or within airport facilities that may affect operations, passenger safety, or regulatory compliance. It applies to all staff, contractors, and third-party vendors operating within the airport environment. The procedures outlined in this policy are designed to ensure accountability, transparency, and effective communication across all levels of the organization.

Operational incidents encompass a wide range of events, including but not limited to equipment failures, safety hazards, security breaches, and disruptions to flight operations. These incidents may result in delays, cancellations, or potential risks to personnel and property. The response to such incidents must be swift, coordinated, and based on established protocols to minimize impact and prevent recurrence. This policy outlines the roles and responsibilities of key personnel, including the Reporting Staff Member, Incident Coordinator, and Airside Duty Manager, to ensure that all incidents are properly documented, investigated, and resolved.

The procedures described in this policy are not limited to the immediate response to an incident but also include a structured review process to identify root causes and implement corrective actions. This review process is essential for maintaining operational integrity and enhancing the airport’s overall safety and service standards. By adhering to this policy, AeroPolicy Airport ensures that all incidents are handled in a consistent and professional manner, fostering a culture of safety and accountability.

## 2. Incident Categories  
Operational incidents are categorized into four primary types: baggage incidents, restricted-item incidents, ramp safety incidents, and equipment incidents. Baggage incidents include events such as lost, damaged, or delayed luggage, as well as any issues related to baggage handling equipment or procedures. Restricted-item incidents involve the discovery of prohibited items, such as weapons, explosives, or other hazardous materials, in airport facilities or on aircraft. Ramp safety incidents refer to any event that compromises the safety of personnel or equipment on the ramp area, including vehicle collisions, unauthorized access, or hazardous material spills. Equipment incidents cover malfunctions, failures, or damage to airport equipment, including ground support equipment, lighting systems, or communication devices.

Each category of incident requires specific handling procedures, which are detailed in the relevant policies. For example, baggage incidents must be reported and escalated in accordance with the procedures outlined in AP-BAG-001, which includes a defined escalation deadline for incident reporting. Restricted-item incidents must be managed under the guidelines provided in AP-SEC-003, which outlines the steps for securing, reporting, and investigating such incidents. The categorization of incidents ensures that appropriate personnel and resources are mobilized to address each type of event effectively and in compliance with regulatory requirements.

The classification of incidents also facilitates a more targeted and efficient response. By identifying the nature of the incident early, the Incident Coordinator can determine the appropriate level of urgency and assign the necessary resources. This structured approach ensures that all incidents are addressed in a timely manner, reducing the potential for further complications and ensuring the safety of all individuals involved.

## 3. Reporting and First Response  
All staff members are required to report any operational incident immediately upon discovery. The Reporting Staff Member is responsible for providing a clear and accurate description of the incident, including the location, time, and any affected personnel or equipment. This initial report should be made to the Airside Duty Manager or the designated Incident Coordinator, depending on the nature and urgency of the incident. The first response should focus on ensuring the safety of personnel and the public, mitigating any immediate risks, and preventing further escalation of the incident.

In the event of an incident that poses a direct threat to safety, the Airside Duty Manager must take immediate action to secure the area and initiate an emergency response. This may include evacuating personnel, shutting down equipment, or coordinating with emergency services if necessary. The Reporting Staff Member should provide any relevant information to support the initial response, including witness accounts, equipment status, and any visible damage. The Incident Coordinator is responsible for assessing the situation and determining the appropriate course of action, including whether to escalate the incident to higher management or external authorities.

The first response to an incident is critical in minimizing its impact and ensuring a swift recovery. By following the procedures outlined in this policy, all personnel can contribute to a safe and efficient operational environment. The prompt and accurate reporting of incidents ensures that the appropriate resources are available to address the situation effectively and prevent similar incidents in the future.

## 4. Incident Coordinator Duties  
The Incident Coordinator must open an incident record for every reported incident before the end of the shift in which it was reported. This record should include a detailed description of the incident, the time and location of occurrence, and the initial response taken. The Incident Coordinator is also responsible for coordinating the investigation and ensuring that all relevant personnel are informed of the incident and their roles in the response. This includes notifying the Airside Duty Manager, the relevant department heads, and any external agencies that may be involved.

The Incident Coordinator must ensure that all incident-related information is accurately documented and stored in the airport’s incident management system. This includes any photographs, video footage, or witness statements that may be relevant to the investigation. The Incident Coordinator is also responsible for tracking the progress of the incident investigation and ensuring that all corrective actions are implemented in a timely manner. This includes coordinating with the appropriate departments to address any equipment failures, procedural lapses, or safety hazards identified during the investigation.

The Incident Coordinator plays a vital role in maintaining the integrity of the incident response process. By ensuring that all incidents are properly documented and investigated, the Incident Coordinator contributes to the continuous improvement of airport operations. This role requires a high level of attention to detail, coordination, and communication to ensure that all incidents are managed in a manner that upholds the safety and efficiency of AeroPolicy Airport.

## 5. Post-Incident Review  
Following the resolution of an incident, a post-incident review must be conducted to determine the root cause and identify corrective actions. This review is led by the Incident Coordinator in collaboration with the Airside Duty Manager and relevant department heads. The review process involves analyzing all available data, including incident reports, witness statements, and any technical assessments, to understand the factors that contributed to the incident. This analysis should be thorough and objective, ensuring that all possible causes are considered.

The post-incident review must result in a detailed report that outlines the cause of the incident, the actions taken to address it, and the corrective measures implemented to prevent recurrence. This report should be reviewed by senior management and shared with all relevant stakeholders. The findings of the review should be used to update procedures, improve training, and enhance safety protocols across the airport. This continuous improvement process is essential for maintaining high standards of operational safety and efficiency.

The review meeting must be documented and recorded, with all participants acknowledging the findings and agreeing on the corrective actions. This documentation serves as a reference for future incident management and is retained for audit purposes. By conducting a structured and comprehensive post-incident review, AeroPolicy Airport ensures that all incidents are not only resolved but also used as opportunities for learning and improvement.

## 6. Records  
All incident records must be maintained in a secure and accessible manner for the duration required by applicable regulations and internal policies. Each incident record must include a detailed description of the incident, the time and location of occurrence, the initial response, and the outcome of the incident. These records must be reviewed and updated as necessary to reflect any changes in the incident’s status or resolution.

The Incident Coordinator is responsible for ensuring that all records are accurately maintained and stored in the airport’s incident management system. Access to these records is restricted to authorized personnel only, and all access must be logged and monitored. The records must be retained for a minimum period of five years, unless a shorter retention period is required by law or regulatory authority.

All incident records must be made available for review by internal auditors, regulatory bodies, and senior management upon request. These records are also used to support training programs and to identify trends that may indicate systemic issues within airport operations. By maintaining accurate and comprehensive records, AeroPolicy Airport ensures transparency, accountability, and continuous improvement in its operational management.
