# AP-INC-002 Operational Incident Response and Review

## 1. Purpose and Scope  
This policy establishes the procedures for reporting, coordinating, and reviewing operational incidents at AeroPolicy Airport. It ensures a consistent and effective response to incidents that may affect safety, security, or operational efficiency. The policy applies to all incidents occurring on airport premises, including those involving baggage, restricted items, ramp safety, and equipment. It defines the roles and responsibilities of reporting staff members, incident coordinators, and airside duty managers. The procedures outlined in this policy are designed to support timely resolution, prevent recurrence, and maintain compliance with applicable regulations and standards. All incidents must be documented and reviewed in accordance with the relevant policies and procedures to ensure accountability and continuous improvement.

## 2. Incident Categories  
Operational incidents are categorized to facilitate appropriate response and review. Baggage incidents include any events involving lost, damaged, or misplaced luggage. Restricted-item incidents involve the discovery or handling of items that are prohibited or restricted by airport security policies. Ramp safety incidents encompass any occurrences that threaten the safety of personnel or aircraft on the ramp area. Equipment incidents refer to malfunctions or failures of airport equipment that may impact operations. Each category requires specific actions and may involve different procedures as outlined in related policies. Baggage incidents must be escalated in accordance with AP-BAG-001, while restricted-item incidents must be handled in accordance with AP-SEC-003.

## 3. Reporting and First Response  
Any staff member who becomes aware of an operational incident must report it to the appropriate supervisor or designated authority. The reporting staff member is responsible for providing a clear and accurate description of the incident, including its location, time, and any potential impact. Upon receiving a report, the airside duty manager or designated supervisor must initiate the first response, ensuring the incident is contained and any immediate risks are mitigated. The incident should be documented and escalated according to the relevant category and policy requirements. All reporting must be done in a manner that ensures accuracy and completeness to support effective incident management and review.

## 4. Incident Coordinator Duties  
The Incident Coordinator is responsible for overseeing the response and resolution of operational incidents. The Incident Coordinator must open an incident record for every reported incident before the end of the shift in which it was reported. This record must include details of the incident, initial actions taken, and any relevant observations. The Incident Coordinator is also responsible for coordinating communication between departments and ensuring that all necessary information is shared with the appropriate personnel. Additionally, the Incident Coordinator must ensure that all incidents are escalated to the appropriate policy or procedure as required by the incident category. The Incident Coordinator must also monitor the progress of incident resolution and ensure that all corrective actions are implemented in a timely manner.

## 5. Post-Incident Review  
Following the resolution of an operational incident, a review meeting must be conducted to evaluate the incident and its response. This meeting includes representatives from relevant departments and is chaired by the Incident Coordinator. The meeting must document the cause of the incident, the actions taken to address it, and any corrective measures implemented. The review must also identify any systemic issues or procedural gaps that contributed to the incident. All findings and recommendations from the review must be recorded and shared with relevant stakeholders to prevent recurrence and improve operational procedures. The review process must be thorough and objective to ensure that all contributing factors are examined and that appropriate measures are taken to prevent similar incidents in the future.

## 6. Records  
All incident-related records must be maintained in a secure and accessible manner. Incident records must include details of the incident, the response actions taken, and the outcome of the post-incident review. These records must be retained for the duration required by applicable regulations and internal audit standards. Access to incident records is restricted to authorized personnel only. All records must be reviewed periodically to ensure their accuracy and completeness. Any discrepancies or incomplete information must be corrected promptly to maintain the integrity of the records. The records must also be made available for internal audits and regulatory inspections to ensure compliance with all applicable requirements.
