# AP-BAG-001 Staff Baggage Handling and Escalation

## 1. Purpose and Scope  
This policy establishes procedures for the handling of checked baggage throughout the airport's baggage handling system. It outlines the responsibilities of staff involved in the movement and management of baggage, ensuring compliance with safety, security, and operational standards. The policy applies to all baggage handlers, baggage duty supervisors, and ramp leads. It ensures that baggage is processed efficiently and that any incidents or irregularities are addressed in a timely and structured manner. This policy supports the safe and secure transportation of baggage while maintaining the integrity of the airport's operations.

## 2. Roles and Responsibilities  
The baggage handler is responsible for the physical handling of baggage between the check-in belts, baggage make-up area, and aircraft loading zones. The baggage duty supervisor oversees the work of baggage handlers and ensures compliance with handling procedures. The ramp lead is responsible for coordinating baggage operations and ensuring that all staff follow established protocols. Each role has specific duties related to the safe and efficient movement of baggage. All staff must report any issues or irregularities to their supervisor. Supervisors are accountable for ensuring that all baggage handling activities are conducted in accordance with this policy and relevant procedures.

## 3. Handling Standards  
Baggage handlers must ensure that all baggage is properly tagged, loaded, and secured during handling. Bag tags must be affixed correctly to ensure accurate identification and tracking. Heavy bags must be handled with care to prevent damage or injury. Fragile items must be clearly marked and handled with extra caution. All baggage must be loaded in accordance with aircraft specifications and weight limits. Handlers must inspect baggage for any signs of damage or tampering before loading. Any discrepancies or concerns must be reported immediately to the baggage duty supervisor. Proper handling procedures help maintain the safety and efficiency of the baggage handling process.

## 4. Baggage Incident Escalation  
When a baggage handler finds a damaged, leaking, or unattended checked bag in the baggage make-up area, the handler must escalate the incident to the Baggage Duty Supervisor within 10 minutes of discovery and must report it under AP-INC-002. All baggage incidents must be documented and reported to ensure proper follow-up. The Baggage Duty Supervisor is responsible for assessing the incident and determining the appropriate action. If necessary, the incident may be escalated to the ramp lead for further review. All staff must ensure that incidents are reported accurately and in a timely manner. This procedure ensures that all baggage issues are addressed promptly and in accordance with established protocols.

## 5. Restricted Items Found in Baggage  
If a restricted item is found in a bag during baggage handling, the bag must not be handled further until it is approved or removed under the restricted-items approval procedure in AP-SEC-003. The baggage handler must immediately notify the Baggage Duty Supervisor upon discovery of the restricted item. The Baggage Duty Supervisor is responsible for initiating the approval process or coordinating the removal of the restricted item. All bags containing restricted items must be isolated and secured to prevent unauthorized access. The handling of such bags must be conducted in accordance with security procedures. This ensures that restricted items are managed in compliance with applicable regulations and airport security policies.

## 6. Records and Review  
All baggage incidents must be recorded in a centralized incident log for review and analysis. The incident log must include details such as the time, location, and nature of the incident, as well as the actions taken. The Baggage Duty Supervisor is responsible for maintaining and updating the incident log. An annual review of this policy is required to ensure its continued relevance and effectiveness. The review process must include input from all relevant staff and management. Any changes to the policy must be communicated to all affected personnel. Regular reviews help ensure that the policy remains aligned with operational needs and regulatory requirements.
