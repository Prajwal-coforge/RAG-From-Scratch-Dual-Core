# AP-INC-002 Operational Incident Response and Review

## 1. Purpose and Scope  
This policy establishes the procedures for reporting, coordinating, and reviewing operational incidents at AeroPolicy Airport. It ensures a consistent and timely response to incidents that may affect safety, security, or operational efficiency. The policy applies to all incidents involving baggage, restricted items, ramp safety, and equipment. It defines the roles and responsibilities of personnel involved in incident management and outlines the process for post-incident review. The goal is to identify root causes, implement corrective actions, and prevent recurrence of similar incidents.

## 2. Incident Categories  
Operational incidents are categorized into four types: baggage, restricted-item, ramp safety, and equipment. Baggage incidents include any event affecting the handling, loss, or damage of baggage. Restricted-item incidents involve the discovery or mishandling of prohibited items. Ramp safety incidents encompass any event that compromises the safety of personnel or aircraft on the ramp. Equipment incidents refer to malfunctions or failures of airport equipment. Each category requires specific procedures for reporting and response, as outlined in relevant policies.

## 3. Reporting and First Response  
All operational incidents must be reported immediately by the affected staff member to their supervisor or the Airside Duty Manager. The Reporting Staff Member is responsible for providing a clear and accurate description of the incident, including time, location, and any immediate actions taken. The Airside Duty Manager ensures that the incident is logged and that appropriate personnel are notified. For baggage incidents, the Reporting Staff Member must refer to AP-BAG-001 section 4 for escalation procedures. For restricted items, the Reporting Staff Member must follow the procedures outlined in AP-SEC-003.

## 4. Incident Coordinator Duties  
The Incident Coordinator is responsible for overseeing the response and resolution of operational incidents. The Incident Coordinator must open an incident record for every reported incident before the end of the shift in which it was reported. This record must include details of the incident, initial response actions, and any relevant documentation. The Incident Coordinator coordinates with all affected departments to ensure a unified approach and facilitates communication with the Airside Duty Manager. The Incident Coordinator also ensures that all necessary information is gathered for the post-incident review.

## 5. Post-Incident Review  
Following the resolution of an incident, a review meeting is conducted to analyze the cause and determine corrective actions. The review meeting is led by the Incident Coordinator and includes representatives from all relevant departments. During the meeting, the cause of the incident is thoroughly examined, and any contributing factors are identified. Corrective actions are developed to address the root cause and prevent recurrence. The meeting also evaluates the effectiveness of the initial response and identifies any gaps in the incident management process. All findings and corrective actions are documented and shared with relevant personnel.

## 6. Records  
All incident records must be maintained in a secure and accessible manner for the duration required by regulatory and internal audit standards. The records include details of the incident, the response actions taken, and the outcomes of the post-incident review. These records are reviewed periodically to ensure their accuracy and completeness. Access to incident records is restricted to authorized personnel only. All records are retained in accordance with applicable legal and operational requirements, and are available for inspection upon request.
