# AP-BAG-001 Staff Baggage Handling and Escalation

## 1. Purpose and Scope  
This policy establishes the procedures and responsibilities for the handling of checked baggage throughout the airport's baggage handling system. It ensures the safe, efficient, and compliant movement of baggage between the check-in belts, baggage make-up area, and aircraft loading zones. This policy applies to all staff involved in baggage handling, including Baggage Handlers, Baggage Duty Supervisors, and Ramp Leads. It outlines the standards for baggage handling, incident escalation, and the management of restricted items found in baggage. Compliance with this policy is essential to maintaining the integrity of the airport’s operations and ensuring the safety and security of all passengers and cargo.

## 2. Roles and Responsibilities  
The Baggage Handler is responsible for the physical handling of checked baggage, including loading, unloading, and sorting. The Baggage Duty Supervisor oversees the operations of Baggage Handlers and ensures adherence to all handling procedures and safety standards. The Ramp Lead is responsible for coordinating baggage handling activities with aircraft loading and ensuring that all baggage is properly secured and accounted for. All staff must follow established procedures, report any anomalies or incidents, and maintain clear communication with their assigned supervisors. Each role has specific duties to ensure the smooth and secure movement of baggage through the airport’s system.

## 3. Handling Standards  
All baggage must be handled with care to prevent damage, loss, or misrouting. Baggage Handlers must ensure that all bags are properly tagged, loaded, and secured during transit. Special attention must be given to heavy bags, which require additional handling precautions to prevent injury or damage. Fragile items must be clearly marked and handled with extra care to avoid breakage. Baggage Duty Supervisors must monitor the handling process to ensure compliance with these standards and provide guidance to Baggage Handlers when necessary. All staff must be trained in the proper handling of different types of baggage and must follow established protocols to maintain the integrity of the baggage handling system.

## 4. Baggage Incident Escalation  
When a baggage handler finds a damaged, leaking, or unattended checked bag in the baggage make-up area, the handler must escalate the incident to the Baggage Duty Supervisor within 10 minutes of discovery and must report it under AP-INC-002. All baggage incidents must be documented and reported promptly to ensure timely resolution. If a baggage handler identifies a potential security risk, they must immediately notify the Baggage Duty Supervisor and follow the procedures outlined in AP-SEC-003. The Ramp Lead is responsible for ensuring that all baggage is properly accounted for and that any discrepancies are addressed before aircraft departure. All staff must be familiar with the escalation procedures and must act in accordance with these guidelines to maintain operational efficiency and safety.

## 5. Restricted Items Found in Baggage  
If a restricted item is found in a bag during baggage handling, the bag must not be further processed until it is approved or removed under the restricted-items approval procedure in AP-SEC-003. Baggage Handlers must immediately notify the Baggage Duty Supervisor upon discovery of any restricted item. The Baggage Duty Supervisor is responsible for initiating the approval process and ensuring that the bag is not released until the appropriate authority has reviewed and approved its handling. All staff must be trained to recognize restricted items and must follow the procedures outlined in AP-SEC-003 to ensure compliance with security and regulatory requirements. Any bag containing a restricted item must be isolated and handled in accordance with the established protocol to prevent unauthorized access or potential threats.

## 6. Records and Review  
All baggage incidents, including damaged, leaking, or unattended bags, must be recorded in the incident log for review and analysis. The incident log must include details such as the time of discovery, location, description of the incident, and actions taken. The Baggage Duty Supervisor is responsible for maintaining and updating the incident log. The policy must be reviewed annually to ensure its continued relevance and effectiveness. All staff must be made aware of the review process and must provide feedback to improve the policy. The annual review must include an assessment of incident trends, handling procedures, and compliance with regulatory requirements. The policy will be updated as necessary to reflect changes in procedures, regulations, or operational needs.
